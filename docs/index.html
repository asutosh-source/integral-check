What the checker does

It integrates your integrand numerically, evaluates the claimed closed form, and compares the two values. A result is labelled NUMERICAL MATCH when the computed value agrees within tolerance and the heuristic error estimate is sufficiently small. This is numerical evidence, not a proof of an identity or of the submitted reasoning.

The browser integrator uses tanh-sinh quadrature in double precision (typically about 15 significant digits at best). Its refinement-difference error estimate is heuristic, not a rigorous bound. Non-finite interior samples are not silently replaced by zero. The checker uses a few explicit diagnostics and recognized special cases; it is not a general convergence prover.

Writing expressions

  • Use x as the variable. Powers can be written x^2 or x**2. Variables other than x are not supported.
  • Functions: sin cos tan asin acos atan sinh cosh tanh exp ln log sqrt abs gamma erf zeta beta li2 digamma (special-function support and domains vary by implementation). log means natural log. Use log10 for base 10.
  • Constants: pi (or π), e, catalan. Bounds may be inf or -inf.
  • Implicit multiplication works: x e^x, 2pi, sin^2 x, √π/2.

Limits

  • It checks the final value only. Whether the reasoning is valid is your judgement, recorded with the grade menu on the Benchmark tab.
  • The standard Dirichlet integral sin(x)/x on [0, ∞) is recognized as a dedicated identity-based special case. This is not a general-purpose oscillatory quadrature method.
  • A few common non-integrable singularities (including 1/x and 1/x² at zero) receive explicit DIVERGENT diagnostics. Arbitrary convergence and divergence cannot be decided reliably by numerical sampling alone.
  • A decimal approximation is not a closed form. If a system gives only a decimal, record it as not answering the question.
